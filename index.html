🛠️ Step-by-Step Low-Cost Fabrication Guide (How to Build with Basic Tools)

This staircase can be fabricated by any local welder using standard workshop tools (angle grinder, electric drill, tape measure, and stick welder). No CNC machines required:

Step 1: Buy Standard Steel Lengths from Local Steel Yard
  • Visit a standard local steel stockist (do not go to a specialty laser cutting shop).
  • Purchase two 6-meter commercial lengths of 100×50×3.0mm RHS box tube (approx $35 each).
  • Purchase one 6-meter length of 40×40×4mm angle iron (approx $18).
  • Purchase one 1-meter offcut piece of 100×6mm flat bar for the mounting plates (approx $8).
Step 2: Cut Tubes & Angle Cleats with an Angle Grinder
  • Cut both 100×50mm box tubes to exactly 4,160 mm length with a 38.6° miter cut at the ends using a standard abrasive cutoff wheel.
  • Cut the 40×40mm angle iron into 26 pieces of 220 mm length each.
  • Drill two 9mm holes through the horizontal leg of each angle iron piece for the tread mounting screws.
  • Cut four pieces of 100×6mm flat bar (150mm long) for the top and bottom mounting plates; drill two 14mm holes in each.
Step 3: Mark Step Positions & Weld Cleats to Box Tubes
  • Lay both box tubes parallel on the workshop floor.
  • Using a carpenter's framing square and chalk/pencil, mark the steps at 185.7 mm vertical rise and 250.0 mm horizontal run.
  • Clamp the 40×40mm angle cleats onto the stringer tubes along the chalk lines.
  • Tack-weld each cleat and check horizontal level with a spirit level.
  • Run a strong 4mm fillet weld along both sides of each angle cleat using a standard stick welder (E6013 welding rods).
  • Weld the top and bottom flat bar mounting plates to the stringer ends.
Step 4: Bolting Stringers On-Site (Zero Concrete!)
  • Carry both stringers to the house (each tube weighs only ~30 kg, easy for one person to carry).
  • Position stringers along the side wall spaced at 800 mm outer width.
  • Bolt top plates directly to the web of the 150×75mm mezzanine steel I-beam with two M12 bolts per stringer.
  • Drill two holes into the ground floor slab per baseplate and install M12 mechanical expansion anchors.
  • Tighten firmly. The frame is 100% rigid with zero concrete curing downtime!
Step 5: Prepare Local Pine Treads & Fasten from Underneath
  • Buy standard 38mm (1.5") thick treated pine, cypress, or local structural timber planks ($5.75 each).
  • Cut 13 pieces to 800 mm width × 250 mm depth. Smooth edges with sandpaper.
  • Apply 2 coats of clear polyurethane floor varnish or wood sealer.
  • Lay treads onto the welded steel angle cleats.
  • From underneath, screw M8 × 40mm hex coach screws with washers through the angle iron holes into the wood.
  • Zero screw heads visible on top; no specialized brass inserts needed!
Step 6: Weld Simple Pipe Handrail & Apply Paint
  • Weld two 32mm round steel pipe uprights to the outer stringer (one at base, one at top).
  • Weld a continuous 32mm round steel pipe handrail along the top at 900mm height above tread nosing.
  • Weld three 16mm round steel safety guard rails horizontally between the uprights (costing just $15).
  • Clean steel with thinner, brush on 1 coat of red oxide primer, and finish with 1 coat of matte black enamel.
  • The staircase is complete, fully functional, and cost only ~$310 USD!

🔨 Step-by-Step Construction Guide (Contractor Manual)

Phase & Step Required Tools Detailed Assembly Instructions Quality Control & Golden Rules
STEP 1: Anchor Plates Rotary hammer drill (18mm SDS bit), chemical resin gun, torque wrench, spirit/laser level Drill four 18mm holes, 200mm deep, into the concrete top of each of the 4 masonry pillars. Blow out dust completely with compressed air. Inject vinylester chemical resin into each hole and rotate M16 threaded studs into place. Let resin cure for 60 minutes. Place 200×200×10mm steel baseplates over studs, level with non-shrink grout, and torque nuts to 120 Nm. Rule: Baseplates must be 100% level with laser alignment across all 4 pillars before tightening. If plates are unlevel, steel beams will twist.
STEP 2: Steel Beams Spanners, socket set, clamps, red oxide primer, metal drill / MIG welder Two workers lift the 150×75mm Universal Beams (~56 kg each) onto the pillar baseplates. Clamp beams into place. Bolt the bottom flanges through pre-drilled holes in the baseplates using high-tensile M16 bolts with spring washers (or run 6mm continuous fillet weld along both sides of flange). Coat all steel surfaces with two coats of anti-corrosion red-oxide primer and matte black enamel. Rule: No crane required! Lightweight structural steel is easily manageable by two people, saving $800+ in crane rental fees.
STEP 3: Timber Joists Chop saw / circular saw, tape measure, hammer, framing square, 38mm twist nails Cut 50×150mm timber joists to exact span length (4,000mm). Mark out steel beams at exactly 400mm (16") centers. Fasten heavy-duty galvanized steel joist hangers at every mark using 38mm twist nails through every hole. Seat timber joists into hangers and nail through side tabs. Install 50×150×350mm solid timber noggins (cross-blocking) in a staggered line at the center of the span (every 1.5m). Rule: Never space joists at 600mm. 400mm spacing plus solid noggins is mandatory to give the floor the rock-solid feel of concrete.
STEP 4: Sound Insulation Insulation serrated knife, dust mask, safety goggles, gloves Friction-fit 75mm high-density Rockwool mineral wool batts snugly between timber joists. Ensure batts fit tightly against joist sides with zero gaps or compressed spots. Drill 20mm holes through the center third of joists to route all flexible PVC electrical conduit pipes for ground ceiling lighting before closing subfloor. Rule: Do not notch the top or bottom edges of joists for wiring! Only drill through the middle neutral axis to preserve full beam strength.
STEP 5: Glue & Screw Subfloor Caulking gun, cordless impact driver with T25 Torx bit, chalk line Apply a generous 6mm continuous bead of polyurethane subfloor adhesive along the top edge of every timber joist. Lay 18mm tongue-and-groove plywood sheets across joists (perpendicular). Tap tongue and groove tightly together using a scrap wood block. Drive 50mm Torx deck screws every 150mm along the edges and every 300mm across intermediate joists, countersinking screw heads 1mm flush. Rule: Never use smooth nails! Screws plus polyurethane adhesive eliminate 100% of timber floor squeaks permanently.
STEP 6: Underlay & Hardwood Utility knife, tapping block, pull bar, spacers, moisture meter Check subfloor moisture (<12%). Roll out 3mm high-density acoustic rubber foam underlay with aluminum vapor barrier facing down; tape all butt joints with vapor barrier tape. Lay 14mm engineered oak hardwood planks parallel to the longest wall. Use 8mm plastic spacers around all perimeter walls to maintain expansion gap. Click planks together tightly using tapping block. Install solid timber skirting baseboards over expansion gap. Rule: Never pin or nail baseboards into the hardwood floorboards; only nail baseboards into the wall studs so the floor can expand freely.
STEP 7: Lower Ceiling Drywall lifter/T-bar props, drywall screw gun, jointing knife, hawk Fasten 12.5mm SoundShield gypsum plasterboards to the underside of the timber joists using 32mm phosphate drywall screws at 200mm centers. Apply self-adhesive fiberglass joint tape over all seams. Skim joints with two coats of joint compound, sand smooth, apply primer, and paint two coats of ceiling acrylic white. Cut 75mm circular holes and snap in flush warm LED downlights. Rule: Stagger plasterboard joints like brickwork to prevent hairline cracks along joist lines.
